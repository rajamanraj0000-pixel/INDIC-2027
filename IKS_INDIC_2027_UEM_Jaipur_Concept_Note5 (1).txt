1.  Background and Rationale
The National Education Policy 2020 mandates the integration of Indian Knowledge Systems into higher education, and the IKS Division of the Ministry of Education has since supported IKS centres, curricula and research across technical institutions. Most of the resulting activity has been concentrated in the humanities. The mathematical, astronomical and engineering traditions of India - the Sulbasutras, the Aryabha?iya and its commentarial tradition, the Bakhshali manuscript, the Kerala school's infinite series, Jyoti?sastra computational methods, metallurgy, water engineering and Vastu geometry - remain comparatively under-served at conference level, even though this is the strand where primary sources are most amenable to rigorous, verifiable scholarship.
Two gaps motivate this conference:
1. A methodological gap. IKS scholarship in the sciences oscillates between uncritical claim-making and outright neglect. There are few forums in which a mathematician, a Sanskritist and a historian of science are in the same room and hold each other to a common evidentiary standard.
2. A pipeline gap. Institutions are being asked to teach IKS content in STEM programmes without a body of peer-reviewed, classroom-ready material grounded in primary texts.
The conference is international by design. Substantial work on Indian mathematics and astronomy is done outside India - in the history-of-science communities of Europe, Japan, the United States and West Asia - and the transmission questions at the heart of the field (Indo-Arabic-European exchange, Kerala-to-Europe priority debates) cannot be settled by a domestic conversation alone.
2.  Aim
To establish a rigorous, source-based international forum for research on the mathematical, scientific and technological knowledge traditions of India, and to convert that research into peer-reviewed publications and teachable curriculum material for higher education.
3.  Objectives
1. Bring together mathematicians, historians of science, Sanskritists, archaeologists and engineering faculty from India and abroad on a shared evidentiary footing.
2. Present and debate current research on Indian mathematical, astronomical and technological traditions drawn from primary sources.
3. Produce teaching-ready modules mapping IKS content onto existing UG and PG syllabi in mathematics, physics, computer science and engineering.
4. Train early-career researchers in source-critical method - manuscript access, editions, translation pitfalls, dating controversies, computational reconstruction.
5. Build durable international collaboration: joint publications, student and faculty exchange, and institutional MoUs arising from the meeting.
6. Establish a standing network and a biennial conference series hosted at UEM Jaipur.


4.  Scope
In scope. Research grounded in primary evidence - Sanskrit, Prakrit, Tamil, Persian and other Indic-language texts; manuscripts and commentaries; inscriptions; archaeological and artefactual material; and computational or experimental reconstruction of documented historical procedures. Comparative and transmission studies involving Greek, Chinese, Islamicate and European traditions are welcome where India is a substantive term in the comparison. Pedagogical and curricular contributions are welcome where they are anchored to identifiable sources.
Standard of review. All submissions are reviewed double-blind by at least two reviewers against ordinary scholarly standards of evidence. This standard is stated openly and is the conference's principal differentiator.
Coverage. Chronologically from the Harappan period to the colonial encounter, with a final track on contemporary application and pedagogy. Geographically the whole of the Indian subcontinent, including regional and vernacular scientific traditions that are typically crowded out by the Sanskrit mainstream.

• Shipbuilding, navigation and maritime technology
Track D - Life Sciences, Agriculture and Environment
• Ayurvedic pharmacology and its contemporary experimental validation
• V?k?ayurveda, traditional agronomy and seed practice

Track E - Language, Logic and Computation

• Mima?sa interpretive rules; sastra as rule-based knowledge representation

Track F - Pedagogy, Curriculum and Digital Humanities
• IKS module design for STEM programmes; credit structures under NEP 2020
• Assessment design and learning outcomes for IKS courses
• Manuscript digitisation, OCR for Indic scripts, corpus building, computational philology

Track G - Methodology, Historiography and Transmission
• Dating and chronology debates; methods and their limits
• Transmission - Indo-Arabic-European exchange, Kerala-to-Europe, India-China contacts
• Colonial, nationalist and revisionist historiography of Indian science
• Standards of evidence in IKS research; critical response to pseudo-historical claims
• Archives, manuscript access and the politics of the source base
6.  About UEM Jaipur
The University of Engineering & Management, Jaipur is a private university located near Sikar Road, Jaipur, Rajasthan, offering undergraduate, postgraduate and doctoral programmes in engineering, management, physiotherapy, computer applications, sciences and allied disciplines. It was established by Ordinance 11 of 2011 and Act No. 5 of 2012 of the Government of Rajasthan. The university is part of the IEM-UEM group, a network of institutions focused on engineering and management education, and emphasises industry-oriented curricula, outcome-based education, research and entrepreneurship.
UEM Jaipur is NAAC-accredited, AICTE-approved and ISO-certified, and holds memberships of the Association of Indian Universities, the Association of Commonwealth Universities (UK), the International Accreditation Organisation and the International Association of Universities (France). It is one of the 200 mentor institutions designated by AICTE under the Ministry of Education. The university's motto, Shraddhavan labhate jnanam, is drawn from the Bhagavad Gita - an apt frame for a conference on India's knowledge traditions.
7.  About Jaipur
Jaipur is the capital of Rajasthan and, for a conference on Indian scientific traditions, is close to an ideal venue - the city is itself a primary source.
• Jantar Mantar. Sawai Jai Singh II's masonry observatory, built in the 1720s-30s and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, contains the world's largest stone sundial and a set of instruments designed for naked-eye positional astronomy of remarkable precision. It is the single best surviving demonstration of the Indian astronomical tradition in its late, instrument-building phase, and sits roughly 25 km from campus. A guided technical visit is built into the programme.
• The city plan. Jaipur was founded in 1727 on a grid derived from Vastu and Silpa Sastra prescriptions - a working case study for Track C, and a rare instance of a classical text being executed at urban scale.
• Wider context. Amber Fort and its water-harvesting systems; the Jaigarh cannon foundry; City Palace and its manuscript holdings; and, within reach, the Zawar zinc-smelting site - one of the earliest known industrial-scale distillation operations anywhere.
• Access. Jaipur International Airport has direct connections to Gulf hubs and, via Delhi (about 4.5 hours by expressway or 20 minutes by air), to the rest of the world. Vande Bharat and other rail services connect Delhi in under four hours. Hotel capacity across all price bands is abundant.
• April conditions. Warm and dry - daytime temperatures typically in the high 30s °C, low humidity, evenings comfortable. Indoor sessions must be air-conditioned; outdoor components should be scheduled early morning or after 17:00. The Jantar Mantar visit is therefore placed at first light on Day 2 or as a post-conference morning excursion.
Jaipur's tourism infrastructure also raises the odds of overseas academics accepting an invitation, and supports accompanying-person and post-conference tour options that cost the organisers nothing.
